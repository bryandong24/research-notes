The matched-source control study tells us *which complete system worked best*. It does not, by
itself, tell us why.

Our leading explanation is stable task tracking:

> The task-specific GIAVA predictor follows the needle and target region smoothly enough to
> keep small external geometry in the tokenizer's fine bands. Generic saliency often shifts
> toward visually conspicuous robot hardware.

This note tests that explanation on a common-observation panel. Every source sees the same 5,000
recorded frames from 20 thread-needle demonstrations. The simulator state and moving camera are
reconstructed frame by frame, task landmarks are projected into the image, and each projected
point is assigned to the exact multiresolution band it would receive.

The analysis explains the control result, but it is not a causal mediation study. GIAVA also
appends gaze numerically to proprioception, and the task-specific predictor likely trained on
some of the public demonstration frames used here.

## Exact GIAVA token geometry

The foveated tokenizer resizes an image to $288\times288$ and extracts approximately 20
$16\times16$ tokens around gaze $g$:

| Band | Token count | Source stride | Spatial support |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Fine core | 4 | 1 | central $32\times32$ |
| Medium ring | 8 | 2 | surrounding region out to $96\times96$ |
| Coarse ring | 8 | 6 | surrounding region out to $288\times288$ |

A point in the stride-1 band is represented at full resized-image density. A stride-2 token
summarizes a source region twice as wide and tall; a stride-6 token summarizes one six times as
wide and tall.

For a projected landmark $p$ and gaze center $g$, define its relative input sampling density as

$$
\rho(p,g)=
\begin{cases}
1, & p\in \mathcal B_1(g),\\
\frac{1}{4}, & p\in \mathcal B_2(g),\\
\frac{1}{36}, & p\in \mathcal B_6(g),\\
0, & p\notin \mathcal B_1(g)\cup\mathcal B_2(g)\cup\mathcal B_6(g).
\end{cases}
$$

Here $\mathcal B_s(g)$ is the ring assigned source stride $s$. The $1/s^2$ weight measures
relative input sampling density, not learned importance and not control value.

This exact-band analysis is stricter than asking whether a landmark lies inside the large clear
square drawn in a video. That square is only an explanatory display.

## Which geometry should be external?

The policy already receives robot joint state. The small external quantities it must infer
visually are therefore especially interesting.

We define a phase-critical external landmark from the task's discrete progress:

- needle center during acquisition;
- needle tip during transport;
- needle tip and target-hole proxy during insertion;
- needle tip during threading and handoff.

These are intentionally narrow diagnostic targets. They do not exhaust useful visual
information: grasp boundaries, collision geometry, the other hand, or future targets may also
matter.

For every frame, the recorded robot and object state is restored in the simulator. If $P$ is a
world-space landmark, $C$ the camera position, and $R$ its camera-to-world rotation, then

$$
P_{\mathrm{cam}}=R^\mathsf{T}(P-C).
$$

With positive depth $d=-P_{\mathrm{cam},z}$ and focal length $f$,

$$
x=W/2+fP_{\mathrm{cam},x}/d,\qquad
y=H/2-fP_{\mathrm{cam},y}/d.
$$

This matters because GIAVA uses an active camera: even a fixed target moves in image
coordinates as the camera arm moves.

## Fine-token allocation

The task-specific predictor nearly matches recorded human gaze in how it allocates resolution
to the phase-critical external landmark.

| Source | Landmark in stride 1 | In stride 1 or 2 | Relative density | Mean distance in 288-space |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Recorded human | 56.5% [52.4, 60.6] | 99.4% [99.0, 99.7] | 0.672 [0.642, 0.703] | 15.74 px |
| GIAVA predicted | **54.6%** [50.1, 59.2] | **99.8%** [99.6, 99.9] | **0.659** [0.625, 0.693] | **15.52 px** |
| Geometric center | 0.0% | 24.0% [15.0, 33.6] | 0.081 [0.061, 0.102] | 61.86 px |
| Training-gaze KDE | 29.7% [26.2, 33.4] | 91.1% [88.7, 93.2] | 0.453 [0.424, 0.482] | 28.90 px |
| DeepGaze-MIT | 26.2% [20.4, 32.6] | 83.1% [79.8, 86.3] | 0.409 [0.362, 0.461] | 36.90 px |
| DeepGaze-uniform | 29.2% [23.1, 35.5] | 62.5% [54.8, 69.9] | 0.385 [0.326, 0.444] | 42.79 px |

On paired episodes, GIAVA predicted gaze exceeds DeepGaze-MIT by 28.4 percentage points of
stride-1 coverage and 0.250 relative sampling density. It exceeds the stationary KDE by 24.9
points and 0.206 density.

The stationary source remains informative because this camera and task have a strong spatial
prior: later activity converges near the target hole. Its weakness appears earlier.

| Source | Acquire | Transport | Insertion | Threaded | Handoff |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Recorded human | 97.9% | 99.8% | 100.0% | 100.0% | 99.7% |
| GIAVA predicted | **99.0%** | **100.0%** | **100.0%** | **100.0%** | **100.0%** |
| Center | 0.0% | 19.6% | 32.9% | 29.0% | 43.7% |
| Training-gaze KDE | 61.0% | 100.0% | 100.0% | 100.0% | 100.0% |
| DeepGaze-MIT | 56.3% | 95.2% | 98.0% | 96.0% | 76.5% |
| DeepGaze-uniform | 67.2% | 70.9% | 94.1% | 65.5% | 33.3% |

Each value is the fraction of phase-critical external landmarks assigned to stride 1 or 2. The
KDE already points toward the eventual goal and therefore performs well after acquisition.
GIAVA predicted gaze adapts to the randomized initial needle location and closes the early-phase
gap without sacrificing later precision.

## Stability at the decision cadence

Correct average placement is not sufficient if the fovea jumps between structures. We sample
every sixth native frame, approximately the cadence at which the policy refreshes its action
chunk and gaze point.

For source $s$, define the gaze displacement

$$
\Delta_t^{(s)}
=
\left\|
p\!\left(g_t^{(s)}\right)
-p\!\left(g_{t-6}^{(s)}\right)
\right\|_2.
$$

| Source | Mean displacement | Displacements over 62 raw pixels |
|---|---:|---:|
| Recorded human | 11.99 px | 3.7% |
| GIAVA predicted | **9.45 px** | **2.8%** |
| Center | 0.00 px | 0.0% |
| Training-gaze KDE | 0.00 px | 0.0% |
| DeepGaze-MIT | 38.37 px | 20.6% |
| DeepGaze-uniform | 59.07 px | 32.7% |

The useful behavior is not movement by itself. The fixed sources are perfectly stable but
cannot retarget. DeepGaze retargets aggressively but often changes which salient structure
receives the fovea. The GIAVA predictor combines low displacement with image-conditioned
tracking.

The current gaze also continues to cover the external geometry over a short future horizon:

| Source | Current stride 1/2 | +0.24 s | +0.48 s |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| GIAVA predicted | 99.8% | 100.0% | 100.0% |
| Training-gaze KDE | 91.1% | 93.4% | 95.5% |
| DeepGaze-MIT | 83.1% | 85.5% | 86.7% |
| DeepGaze-uniform | 62.5% | 64.0% | 66.1% |

This is compatible with the latency horizon of future acquisition-time foveation, but it is not
evidence of anticipation. The needle and target move slowly, and trajectories converge toward a
fixed goal.

## What DeepGaze watches

For each source point, we ask which projected landmark is nearest: external needle/target
geometry, the right gripper-control site, or the left gripper-control site.

| Source | External needle/target | Right gripper | Left gripper |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Recorded human | 99.9% | 0.1% | 0.1% |
| GIAVA predicted | **100.0%** | 0.0% | 0.0% |
| Training-gaze KDE | 97.3% | 0.0% | 2.7% |
| DeepGaze-MIT | 50.2% | **41.2%** | 8.6% |
| DeepGaze-uniform | 55.7% | 13.8% | **30.5%** |

This matches the qualitative videos. Robot hands are large, moving, and conspicuous, which
makes them plausible generic-saliency targets. But their configuration is already partially
specified by the policy's proprioceptive input. Needle and hole state must be inferred visually,
so another increment of spatial resolution may have higher marginal value there.

That explanation remains a hypothesis. The “gripper” points are control-site origins rather
than fingertip segmentations, nearest-point classification is not a causal intervention, and
the policy might still use high-frequency hand appearance.

## The numeric gaze side channel

The gaze point does two jobs:

1. it centers the multiresolution tokenizer;
2. its two coordinates are appended to proprioception.

The second path can expose compact task state even if the image tokens were unchanged. To
measure how much phase information is present in the point alone, we fit a
leave-one-episode-out nearest-centroid phase diagnostic using only $(g_x,g_y)$.

| Source | Balanced phase accuracy from gaze alone |
|---|---:|
| Chance or either constant source | 20.0% |
| DeepGaze-uniform | 36.1% |
| DeepGaze-MIT | 41.4% |
| Recorded human | 56.0% |
| GIAVA predicted | **56.9%** |

This does not show that the action DiT explicitly implements a phase classifier. It shows that
the task-specific point carries more phase information than generic saliency and that the
complete policy has access to it.

Accordingly, the current mechanism claim is:

> The task-specific gaze-conditioned pipeline combines fine allocation to external geometry,
> stable retargeting, and a compact image-derived state coordinate.

The stronger claim—fine token placement alone causes the control advantage—remains unproven.

## The decisive 2×2 experiment

The task-specific dynamic source and stationary KDE already provide the diagonal cells of a
factorial design:

| Tokenizer center | Numeric gaze conditioning | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Dynamic task-specific point | Dynamic task-specific coordinates | existing |
| Dynamic task-specific point | Constant KDE coordinates | new |
| Constant KDE point | Dynamic task-specific coordinates | new |
| Constant KDE point | Constant KDE coordinates | existing |

Training the two missing off-diagonal conditions with the same policy protocol will estimate:

- the effect of dynamic visual allocation while numeric gaze is held constant;
- the effect of dynamic numeric conditioning while token placement is held constant;
- the interaction between the two pathways.

This is the smallest experiment that can turn the current mechanism explanation into a causal
decomposition.

An architecture-controlled label comparison should follow. The present GIAVA-versus-DeepGaze
result changes predictor architecture, preprocessing, readout, supervision, and temporal
behavior. Training identical small U-Nets on human-gaze targets versus cached DeepGaze targets
would isolate label source more cleanly and remove ambiguity about the released gaze model's
training split.

## Broader implication

The mechanism is more specific than “look at the object” and more general than “copy human
gaze.” A useful foveation policy must allocate extra resolution to information that changes the
next action, remain coherent over time, and adapt as the object-target relationship evolves.

That suggests a route beyond task-specific eye tracking:

1. extract action-conditioned relevance from a capable full-resolution VLA or world-action
   model;
2. verify that relevance through matched-budget resolution restoration or deletion;
3. distill it into a small causal selector that sees only low-resolution context at runtime;
4. train the lightweight downstream policy on the selector's actual output distribution.

Human gaze remains the strongest baseline here. It is a teacher to understand and potentially
surpass, not the definition of robot-optimal sensing.

## Limitations

- The mechanism panel contains demonstration states, not the trained policies' own divergent
  rollout states.
- The released task-specific predictor likely trained on some or all of the public
  demonstrations used in the analysis.
- Landmark centers ignore occlusion and do not represent full object extent.
- Phase assignments come from a small reward-derived ontology rather than independent human
  annotation.
- The nearest-landmark and phase-decoding analyses are descriptive rather than causal.
- The current task has one instruction, one camera geometry, and strong spatial regularities.
- All selectors currently see the full-resolution image before choosing gaze, so this remains
  post-acquisition foveation.
