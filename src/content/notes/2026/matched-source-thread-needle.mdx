The gaze-source comparison becomes meaningful only when the downstream action policy is trained
on the same visual distribution that it will receive during rollout.

That is the purpose of this experiment. We trained three complete policies for each of seven
visual conditions, then evaluated every policy on the same 50 simulator initializations. The
result is 150 rollouts per condition and a paired comparison across both policy-training
variation and environment variation.

The main finding is:

> A source-consistent policy using task-specific predicted gaze reached 86.0% exact success,
> compared with 70.0% for generic DeepGaze-MIT and 53.3% for uniformly coarse vision.

This is the primary closed-loop result in the series. It is narrower than “foveation beats full
resolution” and stronger than the earlier frozen source-swap result.

## The policy interface

The action policy does not consume the blurred-square visualization shown in our videos. It
uses GIAVA's multiresolution tokenizer.

Given current image $I$, gaze point $g$, and proprioceptive state $q$, the visual path is

$$
c_{\mathrm{vis}}
=
\operatorname{Pool}
\left(
\operatorname{ViT}\left(T_{\mathrm{fov}}(I,g)\right)
\right).
$$

The image is resized to $288\times288$, and $T_{\mathrm{fov}}$ extracts approximately 20
tokens in three nested bands:

- four stride-1 tokens in a $32\times32$ core;
- eight stride-2 tokens in the surrounding region out to $96\times96$;
- eight stride-6 tokens across the remaining $288\times288$ context.

The policy also receives gaze numerically:

$$
c_{\mathrm{state}}=\operatorname{MLP}([q,g]).
$$

Consequently, the experiment compares complete gaze-conditioned pipelines. It does not yet
isolate high-resolution placement from the information carried by the two gaze coordinates.

An attention pool compresses the ViT sequence into conditioning tokens for an
[action DiT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09748). The action head is trained by
[conditional flow matching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02747). Let $a_1$ be a demonstration
action chunk, $a_0\sim\mathcal N(0,I)$, and $t\sim\mathcal U(0,1)$. With the straight
conditional path

$$
a_t=(1-t)a_0+ta_1,\qquad u_t=a_1-a_0,
$$

the objective is

$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{FM}}
=
\mathbb E
\left[
\left\|
v_\theta(a_t,t\mid c_{\mathrm{vis}},c_{\mathrm{state}})
-u_t
\right\|_2^2
\right].
$$

At rollout, sampling begins from Gaussian noise and integrates the learned vector field in
eight steps. The model predicts a 16-action horizon and executes a short action chunk before
replanning.

## Seven source-consistent conditions

| ID | Visual condition | Gaze during training | Gaze during rollout |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Foveated | task-specific GIAVA U-Net | the same frozen U-Net |
| B | Foveated | geometric center | center |
| C | Foveated | stationary training-gaze KDE mode | the same fixed point |
| D | Foveated | DeepGaze IIE with MIT prior | the same DeepGaze system |
| E | Foveated | DeepGaze IIE with uniform prior | the same DeepGaze system |
| Full | Full-resolution ViT | none | none |
| Low | Uniform low-resolution ViT | none | none |

The foveated conditions use the same multiresolution architecture and pretrained foveated
vision initialization. The full and low conditions use their corresponding released vision
initializations and are therefore contextual anchors rather than architecture-identical
resolution ablations.

For A–E, source resolution precedes the same small Gaussian coordinate noise during training;
noise is disabled during evaluation. The gaze source is frozen. The foveated ViT, attention
pool, projection layers, proprioceptive projection, and action DiT are trainable.

All policies use the same 160 training demonstrations and the same optimization schedule:

- 30,000 updates with batch size 64;
- 500-update warmup followed by cosine decay;
- lower learning rate for the vision backbone than the newly initialized policy layers;
- exponential moving average with decay 0.99;
- action horizon 16 and eight flow-sampling steps;
- no rollout-based model selection—the fixed final EMA weights are evaluated.

The 20 QA and 20 blind demonstrations are excluded from downstream policy training.

## Outcome definition

Thread needle has a discrete five-stage simulator reward based on contact and object state:

1. the right gripper touches the needle;
2. it holds the needle off the table;
3. the needle contacts the wall;
4. the threading condition is triggered;
5. the left gripper holds the threaded needle after the right gripper releases it correctly.

The primary endpoint is exact stage-5 success. Maximum stage and stage-reach rates show where a
policy fails, but they do not replace the success endpoint.

## Token-budget fairness

Foveae near an image boundary can lose valid tokens, so a center-biased source could otherwise
receive a subtle compute advantage. Across A–E, mean valid tokens range from 19.70 to 20.00.
The relative range is 1.51%, well below the predeclared 10% threshold for an equalized-border
control.

That closes one narrow explanation for the source ranking. It does not make Full directly
comparable: Full processes 324 tokens and uses a different pretrained tokenizer.

contextual anchors. The other foveated sources were not evaluated OOD, so the following table
must not be read as a seven-way robustness ranking.

### Compound lighting shift

The lighting suite replaces neutral directional illumination with a randomized-position RGB
spotlight. All nine evaluated policies score 0/50, or 0/150 after aggregation within each
condition.

| Condition | Stage 1 | Stage 2 | Stage 3 | Stage 4 | Stage 5 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A: GIAVA predicted gaze | 10.0% [3.3, 19.3] | 4.0% [0.7, 8.0] | 1.3% [0.0, 4.7] | 0.0% | 0.0% |
| Full-resolution anchor | 47.3% [18.0, 72.0] | 14.7% [4.0, 26.0] | 12.0% [2.0, 23.3] | 0.0% | 0.0% |
| Uniform low-resolution anchor | 0.7% [0.0, 2.7] | 0.0% | 0.0% | 0.0% | 0.0% |

This is a useful negative result, not evidence that the true population success is exactly zero.
The intervention changes color, position, directionality, spotlight geometry, exposure, and
possibly preprocessing behavior simultaneously. The endpoint is floor-censored and cannot
identify which component caused failure.

## What the experiment supports

The cleanest statement is:

> On simulated thread needle, a source-consistent policy using task-specific predicted gaze
> outperformed source-consistent policies using generic saliency, a stationary gaze prior,
> image center, or uniformly coarse tokens.

The result also establishes that the advantage is concentrated in the precision-heavy stages
and is not explained by a meaningful difference in valid foveated token count.

It does **not** establish:

- that recorded human gaze is globally optimal;
- that foveation outperforms full resolution under an architecture-identical comparison;
- that visual placement rather than numeric gaze conditioning caused the entire gain;
- that generic saliency is useless in other robot domains;
- that the system saves camera readout or sensor bandwidth;
- that the ranking generalizes beyond this task, camera, and simulator.

The Full result deserves particular care. A's point estimate exceeds Full, but the two
conditions use different pretrained tokenizers and initializations. Full is an engineering
anchor, not a causal control for the proposition “less visual information is better.”

## Why the task-specific source may help

The rollout funnel suggests that A preserves the small geometry needed for threading and
handoff. But control rates alone do not show what each tokenizer actually resolved.

[The mechanism note](/research-notes/2026/why-task-specific-gaze-works/) projects the needle,
hole, and gripper geometry onto identical observations and assigns each landmark to the exact
stride band it would receive. It also examines a second unresolved pathway: gaze as a compact
numeric task-state signal.
