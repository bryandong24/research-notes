The phrase *gaze source* hides several different objects. A recorded eye-tracker coordinate is
an observation of one person on one demonstration. A gaze predictor is a model that attempts to
reproduce that behavior on a new frame. A saliency model estimates where people tend to look in
ordinary images. A stationary prior encodes where task activity usually occurs without looking
at the current scene at all.

Those signals can correlate, but they are not interchangeable. More importantly, agreement with
human gaze and usefulness to a robot policy are separate properties.

This note follows the first three stages of our GIAVA thread-needle study:

1. validate the spatial and temporal interpretation of the gaze data;
2. compare candidate sources on identical recorded observations;
3. test what happens when an action policy is given a gaze distribution it never saw during
   training.

The final step produces a striking negative result, but not the source ranking one might first
infer from it. That failure is what motivated the matched-source experiment in
[the next note](/research-notes/2026/matched-source-thread-needle/).

## Six different meanings of “where to look”

The dataset stores a left-eye point for the active ZED-left camera. It is an $(x,y)$ coordinate
already normalized to $[-1,1]^2$. For an image of width $W$ and height $H$, the corresponding
pixel coordinate is

$$
p(g)=\frac{g+1}{2}\odot(W,H).
$$

We compare the following sources:

| Source | Definition | Image conditioned? | Valid on a new rollout? |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Recorded human gaze | Eye-tracker point from the demonstration | yes, through the operator | no |
| GIAVA predicted gaze | Task-specific U-Net trained to predict human-like gaze | yes | yes |
| Stationary human-gaze KDE | QA agreement uses a leave-one-episode-out fit to the other 19 QA episodes; policy experiments use a separate fit to Train160 only | no | yes |
| DeepGaze-MIT | DeepGaze IIE with its natural-image spatial prior | yes | yes |
| DeepGaze-uniform | The same DeepGaze weights with a uniform prior | yes | yes |
| Geometric center | Fixed center of the image | no | yes |

The first distinction is operational. Recorded human gaze belongs to the recorded demonstration
trajectory. Once an autonomous policy moves differently, there is no synchronized human gaze to
replay. “Human gaze at rollout” therefore means a learned predictor supervised by human gaze,
not a live human measurement.

The second distinction is semantic. GIAVA's U-Net was trained in this manipulation domain.
DeepGaze is a strong model of generic human visual saliency, but it does not receive the task
instruction, robot state, action history, or manipulation phase. The stationary KDE has even
less information: it emits one fixed point representing where people tended to look in its
fitting split. Two different, noninterchangeable fits appear below. The QA20 label study uses a
leave-one-episode-out (LOEO) KDE fitted separately for each held-out QA episode from the other
19 QA episodes. The closed-loop studies instead use one KDE mode fitted only to the 160
policy-training demonstrations.

## Alignment before metrics

Gaze evaluation is unusually vulnerable to silent errors. Swapping $x$ and $y$, interpreting
$[0,1]$ as $[-1,1]$, flipping the vertical axis, or pairing a gaze sample with the wrong video
frame can all produce plausible-looking overlays while invalidating the experiment.

We therefore treated alignment as an empirical gate. On 20 held-out QA episodes:

- all 5,000 native video rows aligned with the tabular frame indices and timestamps;
- the stored convention was confirmed as $(x,y)$ in $[-1,1]^2$;
- stereo eye separation was primarily horizontal, providing an independent axis-order check;
- the released GIAVA preprocessing reproduced a known model prediction;
- videos were inspected at native 25 Hz before any aggregate metric was trusted.

Passing these gates does not mean that every provided coordinate is a directly observed,
fixation-valid eye-tracker sample. GIAVA fills unlabeled video frames by interpolating between
eye-tracking labels, and the public release does not preserve the original validity or
interpolation mask. “Recorded human gaze” in this note therefore means the released,
frame-aligned trace, which can mix measured samples, saccades or blinks, and interpolated
coordinates.

The downstream comparison uses 200 systematically sampled frames—ten per episode. That sample
is useful for configuration-level behavior but is too sparse to characterize short contacts or
human saccades.

## Agreement with recorded human gaze

Two simple point metrics make the initial comparison tangible.

The first is Euclidean distance from a predicted point $\hat p_t$ to the recorded left-eye point
$p_t$:

$$
d_t=\lVert \hat p_t-p_t\rVert_2.
$$

The second asks whether recorded gaze falls inside a 62-pixel support centered on the source.
The radius represents the outer support of an early foveation proxy, not the finest region of
the eventual GIAVA tokenizer.

| Source | Mean distance to recorded gaze | 62-pixel support hit |
|---|---:|---:|
| GIAVA predicted gaze | **8.49 px** [6.64, 10.59] | **98.5%** [97.0, 100.0] |
| QA20 LOEO human-gaze KDE | 41.08 px [38.99, 43.31] | 72.5% [70.0, 75.0] |
| DeepGaze-MIT | 71.80 px [66.22, 77.85] | 48.0% [41.0, 54.5] |
| DeepGaze-uniform | 86.24 px [77.31, 95.54] | 42.5% [34.0, 50.5] |
| Geometric center | 103.91 px [100.74, 106.90] | 1.5% [0.0, 3.0] |

The center baseline is especially weak because thread-needle activity is usually below image
center in this camera. The stationary KDE captures that task-and-camera prior surprisingly
well. It is substantially closer to human gaze than either DeepGaze variant despite never
responding to the current image.

Density metrics show why this does not make DeepGaze meaningless. On a common probability grid:

| Density source | NSS | AUC-Judd | shuffled AUC |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| DeepGaze-MIT | 3.617 | 0.9698 | 0.7022 |
| DeepGaze-uniform | 3.511 | 0.9672 | 0.7656 |
| QA20 LOEO human-gaze KDE | **6.582** | **0.9881** | 0.4943 |

Normalized scanpath saliency, or NSS, measures the standardized density value at human
fixations. Shuffled AUC uses fixations from other episodes as negatives, suppressing spatial
priors shared across the task. The stationary KDE is consequently excellent on NSS but near
chance on shuffled AUC. DeepGaze captures image-dependent saliency beyond the fixed prior, yet
its saliency is less precisely aligned with task-directed human gaze.

The conclusion is not “DeepGaze sees nothing.” It is:

> Generic image saliency contains scene-responsive information, but that information is not the
> same as task-directed manipulation gaze.

## Temporal stability

A foveation source is a trajectory, not just a set of independent points. If the selected
location jumps between salient structures, the robot repeatedly reallocates fine tokens and may
lose the geometry it was tracking.

At the one-second QA sampling interval:

| Point track | Mean step | Steps larger than 62 px |
|---|---:|---:|
| Recorded human | 28.97 px | 14.4% |
| GIAVA predicted gaze | 27.45 px | 12.8% |
| DeepGaze-MIT | 69.05 px | 43.9% |
| DeepGaze-uniform | 102.53 px | 62.2% |

These are not native-rate saccade measurements. They show configuration-level instability:
DeepGaze's maximum-density point often moves between robot hands, links, and other conspicuous
structures, while the task-specific trace more closely follows the recorded operator.

A lead-lag sweep did not justify an anticipation claim. DeepGaze became closer to later human
gaze at one offset, but center and the stationary prior improved in the same direction. That
shared trend could reflect task progression or camera motion rather than DeepGaze predicting
where a person will look next.

## Does the fovea contain task geometry?

Human agreement is only one axis. A robot may benefit from a non-human fixation if it preserves
the information needed for control.

We therefore built a policy-free geometry assay from the simulator. For every evaluated frame,
the recorded robot, object, and active-camera state was restored. The environment's discrete
contact reward was converted into a monotone progress proxy:

$$
z_t=\max_{\tau\le t} r_\tau.
$$

Each progress stage selected two predeclared landmarks—for example, the right gripper and needle
during acquisition, or the needle tip and target-hole proxy during insertion. The landmarks
were projected through the moving camera into the image. A source received a hit when its
62-pixel circle contained a selected projected center.

The circle corresponds to the nominal sampling fraction

$$
B=
\frac{\pi r^2}{WH}
+
\left(1-\frac{\pi r^2}{WH}\right)\frac{1}{16}.
$$

At $r=62$, $W=640$, and $H=480$, $B=9.94\%$. This is an analytic dense-plus-coarse sampling
budget, not measured sensor cost and not GIAVA's literal token geometry.

| Fovea source | Phase-relevant landmark recall | Both landmarks in one circle |
|---|---:|---:|
| Recorded human | **46.3%** [43.5, 49.0] | 11.5% |
| GIAVA predicted gaze | **46.3%** [43.2, 49.2] | 12.0% |
| DeepGaze-uniform | 38.5% [33.3, 44.0] | 11.5% |
| DeepGaze-MIT | 36.5% [31.8, 41.5] | 10.0% |
| QA20 LOEO human-gaze KDE | 32.3% [29.5, 35.0] | 5.5% |
| Geometric center | 5.8% [4.0, 7.7] | 0.5% |

This table changes the ordering. The stationary KDE is a better predictor of average recorded
gaze than DeepGaze, but DeepGaze covers more of the small phase-dependent landmark set. The KDE
is concentrated near the eventual target: it is excellent during insertion and poor during
initial acquisition.

The assay also exposes a limitation of a single point. Even recorded human gaze places both
selected landmarks inside one primary circle on only 11.5% of frames. Object-target
interactions may require multiple foveae or learned allocation across scales.

This geometry study is diagnostic, not a definition of optimal gaze. Its phase labels are
derived from simulator reward, its landmark ontology is incomplete, projected centers ignore
occlusion, and containment does not prove that resolving the point improves an action.

## The frozen source-swap experiment

The first closed-loop test trained one action policy with the task-specific GIAVA predictor and
then froze it. During evaluation, only the gaze source was changed.

| Fovea supplied at rollout | Exact success | Mean maximum stage |
|---|---:|---:|
| GIAVA predicted gaze | **45/50, 90.0%** | **4.82** |
| Geometric center | 0/50 | 0.00 |
| Train160 KDE mode | 0/50 | 0.00 |
| DeepGaze-MIT | 0/50 | 0.00 |
| DeepGaze-uniform | 0/50 | 0.00 |

The alternatives did not merely miss the final handoff; none reached the first reward stage.
The result was unchanged when the substituted point was supplied to both token placement and
the policy's numeric gaze input. Effective valid-token counts were nearly equal, so a center
token-count advantage did not explain the collapse.

It would be tempting to call this a decisive gaze ranking. It is not.

The action policy was trained only under the moving GIAVA-U-Net foveation distribution. The KDE
substitution here is the fixed mode fitted to Train160—not the QA20 LOEO diagnostic above.
Every alternative changed the distribution of visual tokens at test time, and all four fell to
the same measurement floor. The experiment establishes **source-swap brittleness**:

> A policy can depend strongly on its training-time foveation distribution, so zero-shot gaze
> replacement does not estimate what an alternative source could achieve after matched
> training.

That is why the next experiment trains a complete action policy for each visual regime while
holding the policy recipe fixed.

## What this stage establishes

Three evaluation axes should remain separate:

1. **Human-gaze agreement:** does a source resemble the recorded operator?
2. **Task-geometry coverage:** does its support contain a small set of phase-relevant landmarks?
3. **Closed-loop utility:** can a policy trained with that source complete the task?

GIAVA predicted gaze is strong on the first two axes. DeepGaze is meaningfully
image-conditioned but weaker on task alignment and stability. The stationary KDE captures a
powerful camera prior but cannot track changing geometry. None of those observations alone
determines achievable control performance.

The matched-source control study supplies that missing third axis.

## Limitations

- The released task-specific U-Net likely trained on the same public demonstrations used in
  this QA panel. Its agreement is an implementation and mechanism check, not clean held-out
  generalization.
- Recorded gaze is not necessarily robot-optimal. Human peripheral vision, anticipation, and
  task knowledge may differ from what a robot policy needs.
- The released human trace contains interpolated unlabeled frames, and no original
  fixation-validity or interpolation mask is available. Finite/range checks cannot separate
  direct measurements from those filled values.
- DeepGaze and GIAVA differ in architecture, training data, preprocessing, and point readout.
  This is an operational system comparison, not an architecture-controlled label experiment.
- The circular support analysis uses privileged simulator geometry and is not the actual
  multiresolution tokenizer.
- All current selectors inspect the current full-resolution frame. This is post-acquisition
  allocation, not an acquisition-time bandwidth result.
